Affogato means "drowned" in Italian, and that is exactly what happens: a scoop of gelato disappears under a shot of hot espresso. It belongs to the Italian tradition of simple desserts built from very few ingredients, and in Italy it is found both at the bar and at the end of a meal in restaurants. Its appeal lies in contrast: hot and cold, bitter and sweet, liquid and creamy, all in the same cup.

### How it is made

A scoop of vanilla gelato is placed in a cup or small glass, and an espresso is poured over it just before serving. The heat melts the edges of the gelato, which blends with the coffee into a creamy sauce, while the centre stays cold for a little longer. Timing matters: it is put together at the last moment and eaten straight away.

### Curiosities

- Vanilla and fior di latte are the classic flavours, because they let the coffee speak.
- Some Italians add a splash of liqueur, in the same spirit as the caffè corretto, the espresso "corrected" with a spirit.

### How to enjoy it

The Americano is espresso stretched with hot water to come closer to the long, filtered coffee many visitors were used to. The most common story links its name to American soldiers in Italy during the Second World War, who found espresso too strong and asked for it to be diluted. Whether or not the anecdote is exact, the name stuck, and today the Americano is on the menu of cafés all over the world.

### How it is made

A shot of espresso is extracted as usual, then combined with hot water in a larger cup. The water softens the intensity and makes the drink longer, while the espresso keeps its roasted aroma. Some baristas add the water first and pour the espresso on top to preserve part of the crema; others do the opposite.

### Curiosities

- In Italy it is sometimes confused with the caffè lungo, which is an espresso extracted with more water through the coffee itself, not diluted afterwards.
- "Americano" is also the name of a classic Italian aperitivo cocktail, a completely different drink.
